### ICS Unpacked (Holcomb PDF and ICS attack history)
- **Covered:** Read the whole Holcomb PDF. History Q&A on Davis-Besse (2003, SQL Slammer), Stuxnet (2010) plus LOPA, Ukraine (2015 and 2016), TRISIS/Triton (2017), and Colonial Pipeline (2021).
- **Davis-Besse:** Slammer reached the plant through a contractor's direct line that bypassed the firewall and knocked out the safety parameter display for about 5 hours (the reactor was already shut down). Slammer was a single 376-byte UDP packet on port 1434 exploiting a SQL Server flaw patched about 6 months earlier; MFA wouldn't have helped because it never logged in. Defenses: an inventory of every connection into the plant, all routed through the firewall and DMZ; default-deny rules; separate business and process networks; no internet-exposed servers; flood monitoring; compensating controls when patching is slow. Passive monitoring would detect it fast but not block it, so its value is a fast response.
- **Stuxnet and LOPA:** Infected USB drives crossed a real air gap, hunted the Step 7 engineering workstation, changed PLC logic, and replayed normal readings to the HMI. When software can lie, validate with independent out-of-band sensors, and keep protection layers diverse, focusing that cost on the worst-consequence (safety) layers. LOPA compares initiating-event frequency times each independent layer's failure chance against the tolerable risk target, and the gap sets the SIS SIL (for example 0.1/yr x 0.1 x 0.01 = 1e-4/yr against a 1e-5 target means an SIL 1 SIS). A layer only counts if it's independent.
- **Ukraine:** In 2015, phishing planted BlackEnergy, stolen credentials and the utility VPN let attackers open breakers from the HMI (about 225k customers out for 1-6h), and recovery was sabotaged with a wiper and bricked serial converters. Defenses: MFA on the VPN, a DMZ jump host, separate OT logins, odd-hour login alerts, a response plan. In 2016, Industroyer spoke IEC 104/61850 to open breakers automatically and targeted protective relays. Defenses: independent or hardwired SIS, firewall rules on port and source, passive monitoring for unusual commands.
- **TRISIS and Colonial:** TRISIS went IT to OT to the SIS engineering workstation and reprogrammed safety controllers; it was caught when a bug tripped a safe shutdown, and the physical key switch was reportedly left in PROGRAM. Colonial's ransomware came in through an old VPN account with no MFA and hit business systems, not OT, yet the pipeline shut for about 5 days because it couldn't bill or confirm OT was clean. Plan for "can we operate safely if Level 4 is gone?"
- **Ten steps highlights:** Think like an engineer (safety, then availability, integrity, confidentiality; patch only with vendor approval, testing on a copy, a planned outage, and compensating controls). New terms: IACS, MES. Standards vs regulations: IEC 62443, NIST 800-82, NERC CIP for the bulk grid, Ontario Energy Board rules for local distributors, and Bill C-8 for federal sectors. Steps 5-10 (lab, community, staying current, mentoring, soft skills, 62443 certification) are already on the roadmap.
- **Takeaways:** A contractor's trusted path can bypass an "air gap." Defense is prevent, detect, and respond. Once a flaw and sample code are public, slow patching leaves a window for anyone.
- **Decisions:** None; no new roadmap items. Books added to read: *Sandworm* (Greenberg) and *Countdown to Zero Day* (Zetter).
- **Next:** Continue the roadmap.
